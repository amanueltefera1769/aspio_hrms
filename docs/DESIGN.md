# Aspio HR design

The website makes everyday HR feel organized: a warm paper surface, Aspio purple, large readable headings and product-shaped details rather than invented social proof.

Primary visitors are business owners, HR teams and managers. Their journey is to understand the product, explore the relevant capability and request a demo. Mobile preserves this order and keeps the primary action in the header.

The landing page uses bold Ethiopia-inspired type, a pale grid hero, an interactive HR workspace and a dark multi-branch network. Product illustrations are code-native, with labeled example data. The supplied campaign graphics are feature references, not assets for Home, Services or About. Contact retains its previous design pending a separate redesign. Services explains the actual capabilities without implying biometric compatibility with every device or a full payroll engine. Prices and contracts depend on the business’s package and agreement.

Home, Services and About share hr-prefixed primitives from src/app/home.css. Services-specific layouts live in src/app/services/services.css under svc-prefixed selectors; About owns abt-prefixed styles in src/app/about/about.css. Contact is unaffected by these page-owned styles. Primary journey: understand the product, explore a capability, request a demo. Keep primary conversion visible before the preview on mobile. Do not add invented customer numbers, testimonials or commercial promises.

Services is a browsable feature guide. Its numbered feature rail tracks reading position, preserves existing feature anchors, and scrolls horizontally on small screens. The hero's interactive scene switches between people, attendance and reports, using local example data. Attendance must explicitly show a biometric terminal and a mobile app, not just a dashboard or an abstract slogan. AttendanceDevices owns those code-native device illustrations and working reader/app buttons; both update a shared sample receipt. The remaining stories reuse HomePreviews. The main section order is attendance, chat, payroll preparation, reports, branches, other tools, FAQs and demo request. Keep the real Aspio logo in the hero and mobile screen. Test Services at 320, 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920px, including direct hash links, keyboard use, active-section updates and the demo journey. Check floating illustrations do not cover text or controls.

About is an editorial page: product purpose, principles, a connected-workday narrative and a direct invitation to talk. Its hero follows the supplied About reference: “A clearer workday. A more connected team.”, short supporting copy, a Discover Aspio anchor, and a full-bleed right visual separated by a sweeping white curve. Use the user's supplied image at /images/about/connections-v1.png: two colleagues viewing staff records on a laptop. This replaces the previous abstract artwork and supersedes the earlier no-people direction. Preserve the supplied original; do not identify the pictured people as Aspio employees. Do not reuse campus imagery, caption cards or a decorative belief strip. Mobile places the message and discovery action before the image; reduced motion disables the link-arrow transition. Header differences stay conditional on About. Keep company history, customer numbers, employee identities and performance claims out unless supplied. Public contact details from site.ts belong in the closing section. Test the purpose anchor, Services feature links, email links and demo journey on desktop and mobile.

Demo requests omit the description field and agreement checkbox. ContactForm sends an explicit formType so the server accepts a demo without a message while preserving the required message on Contact. Do not invent a message attributed to the visitor; error-state email links must still contain the submitted contact details. Do not silently grant consent: Demo omits the consent payload and records no separate checkbox agreement; notifications describe contact details supplied to arrange the requested demo. Contact still requires its agreement checkbox. Required identity fields, honeypot and size limits remain enforced for both forms.

The hero screen has seven working preview views, branch filtering, sample check-ins, staff search and profiles, local-only chat, file previews and report periods. Its branding uses the real Aspio logo. Desktop sidebar controls become horizontal navigation on mobile. All interaction remains local sample data and must never be presented as a live HR session. Hero controls and tool layouts are owned by src/components/home/HeroWorkspace.tsx and hero-preview.css.

Use the shared Header, Footer, DemoCallout, Icon and FeatureArtwork components. Keep editable product copy in src/lib/site.ts. Avoid adding invented customer counts, logos, testimonials, product URLs or performance claims.

Navigation, buttons, native dialogs, FAQ disclosure controls and labeled form fields must work by keyboard. Maintain visible focus, useful error feedback, preserved form values and reduced-motion support. Test at 390px and 1440px; inspect complete pages and check 320px and 768px for overflow.

This folder owns its complete runtime. It must not import modules from the Ethiopia website or copy its secrets.

`/home-lab` is an isolated Home duplicate for a connected-workplace hero exploration. Keep original Home unchanged. Its current hero is `MotionCampusHero.tsx`, with mc-prefixed CSS and mechanical crops of the newly supplied campus/reception references. This is a no-credit 2D motion preview, not real-time 3D or generated video. Only Attendance has an animated sequence; Staff records and Team communication link to their Services anchors. Preserve supported-device and payroll-preparation qualifications, labeled example data, reversible user-triggered motion, native keyboard controls and reduced-motion support. No scroll trapping, fake live HR activity or background automatic tours. Keep shared-header differences conditional on the experimental route. See `motion-campus-preview.md` for source provenance, scope and six-width verification. Older 3D and generated-image experiments remain unused by the current route.
